Research

Scientific direction

The website is organized around two main research lines, plus a computational layer that supports both.

Galaxy clusters

Jellyfish galaxies and ram-pressure stripping

I analyze jellyfish galaxy candidates in X-ray selected clusters using wide-field optical imaging, citizen-science morphology, photometric and spectroscopic membership, and environmental proxies of the intracluster medium.

Halo–galaxy connection

Halo spin from spin–orbit coherence

My PhD project develops observational proxies for dark-matter halo spin by measuring the coherence between halo angular momentum and satellite orbital motion in projected phase-space maps.

Large-scale structure

Environment, dynamics, and assembly

I am interested in how coherent motions, cluster environments, and large-scale structure encode information about halo assembly, galaxy evolution, and secondary bias.

Project windows

Current projects

Two complementary research programs linking galaxy evolution, dark-matter halo dynamics, and cosmological environments.

Project 01

Jellyfish galaxies in X-ray selected clusters

This project studies ram-pressure stripping candidates selected from optical morphology and cluster membership information. The central goal is to quantify how jellyfish fractions, radial distributions, and tail orientations depend on host-cluster mass and intracluster-medium structure.

  • Citizen-science morphology calibrated with expert classifications.
  • Photometric and spectroscopic membership selection.
  • Environmental trends with host mass, core radius, temperature, and ICM proxies.
Project 02

Measuring halo spin using spin–orbit coherence

This PhD line explores whether the projected orbital motion of satellite galaxies can be used as an observational proxy for dark-matter halo spin. The methodology combines simulations, phase-space maps, template matching, likelihood weighting, and uncertainty estimation.

  • Simulation calibration with DICE, Uchuu, and TNG-oriented pipelines.
  • Projected line-of-sight velocity maps, spin–orbit coherence estimators, and inclination tests.
  • Statistical calibration of spin inference and tests of secondary halo bias.

Beyond the CV

About Me

A little about where I come from, what drives my curiosity, and life beyond astronomy.

I am an astrophysicist born in Calama, Chile, a city surrounded by the Atacama Desert and renowned for its extraordinary night skies. Growing up beneath these skies, I developed an early curiosity about the Universe: how everything came to exist, how it evolves, and what lies beyond what we can see. Astronomy gradually became a way to explore these questions.

During my university years, I developed the scientific, computational, and communication skills needed to turn curiosity into research. Galaxies and cosmology became my main scientific interests, particularly the connection between dark matter, the formation of cosmic structures, and the evolution of galaxies. Today, as a PhD student in Physical Sciences at Universidad Técnica Federico Santa María and Pontificia Universidad Católica de Valparaíso, I investigate these questions by combining astronomical observations, cosmological simulations, and statistical methods.

Beyond research, I believe that a fulfilling life emerges from the coexistence of curiosity, meaningful work, personal relationships, and the freedom to explore different interests. I enjoy cooking healthy and balanced meals, practicing calisthenics and strength training, playing football, and discovering natural landscapes through trekking and photography. Music is another important part of my life, particularly playing the guitar or practicing improvisation of Rap. I also enjoy conversations about philosophy, human experience, and the questions that shape the way we understand our lives.

More recently, I have become increasingly involved in science communication and astronomy education. I am interested in developing accessible astronomy workshops and educational experiences, as well as exploring projects that combine astronomy, nature, and tourism through activities such as astronomical trekking.

Ultimately, I see science not only as a profession, but also as an expression of a broader curiosity about the world. I hope to contribute to our understanding of the Universe while continuing to discover, learn, and experience the many other things that make life meaningful.
