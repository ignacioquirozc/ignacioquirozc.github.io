Research

Scientific direction

The website is organized around two main research lines, plus a computational layer that supports both.

Galaxy clusters

Jellyfish galaxies and ram-pressure stripping

I analyze jellyfish galaxy candidates in X-ray selected clusters using wide-field optical imaging, citizen-science morphology, photometric and spectroscopic membership, and environmental proxies of the intracluster medium.

Halo–galaxy connection

Halo spin from spin–orbit coherence

My PhD project develops observational proxies for dark-matter halo spin by measuring the coherence between halo angular momentum and satellite orbital motion in projected phase-space maps.

Large-scale structure

Environment, dynamics, and assembly

I am interested in how coherent motions, cluster environments, and large-scale structure encode information about halo assembly, galaxy evolution, and secondary bias.

Project 01

Jellyfish galaxies in X-ray selected clusters

This project studies ram-pressure stripping candidates selected from optical morphology and cluster membership information. The central goal is to quantify how jellyfish fractions, radial distributions, and tail orientations depend on host-cluster mass and intracluster-medium structure.

  • Citizen-science morphology calibrated with expert classifications.
  • Photometric and spectroscopic membership selection.
  • Environmental trends with host mass, core radius, temperature, and ICM proxies.
Project 02

Measuring halo spin using spin–orbit coherence

This PhD line explores whether the projected orbital motion of satellite galaxies can be used as an observational proxy for dark-matter halo spin. The methodology combines simulations, phase-space maps, template matching, likelihood weighting, and uncertainty estimation.

  • Simulation calibration with DICE, Uchuu, and TNG-oriented pipelines.
  • Velocity maps and CDF/mean estimators in projected phase space.
  • Prediction PDFs for spin parameters using score- or chi-squared-based weights.
